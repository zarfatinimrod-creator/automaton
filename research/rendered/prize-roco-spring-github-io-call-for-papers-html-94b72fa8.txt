Call for Papers — RoCo-Spring

Call for Papers

Robust Correspondence Under Realistic Visual Corruptions

We invite concise workshop papers on robust Optical Flow, Stereo Matching,
Scene Flow, and rigorous exploration of robustness in dense correspondence.

Four Tracks

What We Invite

Quantitative
Optical Flow

Methods that estimate dense 2D motion between consecutive frames and remain accurate
under realistic visual corruptions.

Quantitative
Stereo Matching

Methods that estimate dense disparity from rectified stereo pairs robustly across
clean and corrupted imagery.

Quantitative
Scene Flow

Methods that estimate dense 3D motion from stereo video while preserving geometry
and temporal accuracy under corruptions.

Research
Exploration Track

Focused, reproducible studies of failure modes, evaluation, uncertainty,
adaptation, temporal consistency, or robustness trade-offs.

Submission Requirements

Format and Review

Every quantitative or Exploration Track entry must be accompanied by a workshop paper.

Challenge Teams: Identify Your Entry

Include your exact registered team number and team name
in the paper abstract—for example, RoCo-XX · Example Team .

Format: Use the official
NeurIPS 2026 paper template .
Select \usepackage[sglblindworkshop]{neurips_2026} and set
\workshoptitle{RoCo-Spring: The Robust Correspondence Challenge} .

Length: The main paper must be 4–6 pages. References and an optional
appendix may follow and do not count toward the 4–6-page main-paper limit.

Review: Submissions are single-blind; author identities remain visible to
reviewers.

Content: Quantitative papers should report the method, data, compute,
results, ablations, and limitations. Exploration papers should present a focused question,
code, evidence, and reproduction instructions.

Acceptance is based on technical soundness, clarity, reproducibility, and relevance to robust
dense correspondence—not leaderboard rank alone.

Camera-Ready

Reproducibility Package

With the camera-ready paper, submit code and concise reproduction instructions that include:

A declaration listing every training, pretraining, fine-tuning, and validation dataset.

Runnable training and evaluation scripts.

GPU type and count, peak memory, wall-clock time, and other compute requirements.

A README with a pinned environment via pyproject.toml or explicit conda packages
and versions.

For the camera-ready version, add the template's final option. Award eligibility
requires an accepted paper and a complete reproducibility package.
