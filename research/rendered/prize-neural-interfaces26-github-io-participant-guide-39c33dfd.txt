One shared stack · several valid routes
Your participant guide.

Go from choosing a track to submitting a trained model. Develop with NeuralBench, Benchopt, both, or your own pipeline, then submit the trained model to Codabench for official evaluation. Each track provides two complementary worked examples: a NeuralBench start kit to explore the task and data through a neuro-specific model-development and evaluation framework, and a Benchopt competition kit to develop and validate against the submission workflow. Both are recommended, not required.

01

Connected tools · distinct responsibilities
Understand the competition ecosystem.

Three complementary tools support different parts of your path from public data to the leaderboard. NeuralBench and Benchopt are strongly recommended but optional. Codabench is required for registration, submission, and official evaluation.

01 · Tasks, data & baselines
NeuralBench

Neurophysiology task and model environment.
Defines the public tasks, datasets, preprocessing, splits, and reference baselines. Use it to explore a task and run a catalogue model — a task-specific network or a pretrained foundation model (probed or fine-tuned).

Open the NeuralBench guides ↗

02 · Develop and package
Benchopt

Reproducible experimentation and competition preparation.
Use it to develop your own model and compare methods reproducibly. A new architecture lives in your submission.py as plain PyTorch; Benchopt reuses the NeuralBench task and data layer to train, validate, and package it into a submission.

Open the Benchopt competition kit ↗

03 · Submit and score
Codabench

Official participation and evaluation platform.
Use it to register, read the current submission requirements, upload your trained model package, and receive official scores on the track leaderboards.

Open a Codabench track portal ↓

02

Optional but recommended · two complementary entry points
Start with worked examples.

Each track offers a NeuralBench start kit to explore the task and test a model, plus a Benchopt competition kit to exercise the competition workflow and submission contract. Try the entry point closest to your immediate goal. This does not lock in how you train your final model.

03

Your model · three development routes
Choose where to develop and train your model.

All three routes can use your own architecture. Choose according to the workflow you need.

NeuralBench

Use the neuro-specific environment.
Best for developing within neuro-specific tasks, data pipelines, preprocessing, metrics, adaptation strategies, and scientific comparisons. Train or evaluate a built-in or custom PyTorch model, then keep the trained checkpoint.

Develop with NeuralBench ↗

Benchopt

Work directly against the competition.
Best for developing directly against the submission contract with the same core benchmark code Codabench runs. Add your own method, compare configurations, train on public data, and export a submission folder. On real data, Benchopt reuses the NeuralBench task and data layer.

Develop with Benchopt ↗

Independent path

Use your own pipeline.
Train with your preferred codebase and permitted data. Neither NeuralBench nor Benchopt must control training, provided you save everything needed to reproduce inference and satisfy the track contract.

Review track requirements →

These routes are complementary, not cumulative requirements. You can develop in NeuralBench and use Benchopt only to validate and package the result, develop and package directly in Benchopt, train in your own pipeline and use Benchopt only for final validation, or handle training, contract validation, and packaging entirely yourself. You never need to train the same model twice.

Same destination: whatever the training path, you upload a trained model to Codabench. ↓

04

From trained model to leaderboard
Package and submit on Codabench.

Whatever training route you used, the final artifact is the same: a trained model wrapped in the required submission class, called a Solver . Before packaging it, read the shared submission guide and open Get Started → Submission Guide on your Codabench track below . They define how to package, validate, and upload it.

1 Read the contract Open Get Started → Submission Guide on your Codabench track.

2 Build the submission Wrap the architecture, inference preprocessing, trained weights, and any optional files in the required Solver .

3 Validate and ZIP Test inference locally with Benchopt, then zip the contents of the submission folder.

4 Upload and check Submit through My Submissions and confirm that evaluation succeeds.

Two evaluation stages: Warm-up scores use public test data and are indicative. Codabench switches to the private 2026 cohorts for the sealed phase, and only sealed scores determine the final ranking.
