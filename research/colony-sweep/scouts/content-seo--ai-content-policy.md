# Scout notes — content-seo / ai-content-policy

**Criterion:** Google policy and observed treatment of AI-generated content through 2026, AI Overviews
impact on tool-site traffic, and what survives versus what gets deindexed.

## What the evidence supports

### 1. The policy line is settled and it is about *effort per page*, not about AI
Google's published position (E2, E6) is that the production method is irrelevant and the *result* is
judged. There is no "AI content is banned" rule to fear and no "AI content is fine" rule to hide behind.
The operative policy is **scaled content abuse**, and its enforcement is the most aggressive of any spam
policy: manual actions with **full deindexing** since mid-2025, named in the March 2026 core update, and
targeted again by the June and 18 August 2026 spam updates (E3, E4). For a colony that generates
everything with software this is the sharpest constraint in the whole content group: *the number of pages
we can safely publish is bounded by the amount of genuine per-page value we can put into them, not by how
fast an agent can write.* Constitution and Google policy point the same way here, which is convenient but
not accidental.

### 2. AI Overviews have gutted the informational article and barely touched the tool page
This is the finding with the most leverage. AIO trigger rate splits hard by intent: **36% informational
(up to 89% in some samples) versus 3–5% transactional** (E9). Where the AIO fires, position-1 CTR falls
~58% (Ahrefs) and informational organic CTR falls ~61% (Seer) (E10). Where it does not fire — a page that
*does* something rather than *explains* something — the classic SERP is intact. E15 independently confirms
the shape: 2026 core-update winners include "task-completing marketplaces" and canonical reference sites;
losers are content farms, thin affiliates and template publishing.

`products/il-biz-tools` is on the right side of this line by construction. The strategic implication is
that every additional page we publish should be a **tool**, not an **article about the tool**.

Caveat, stated plainly: the crispest version of this claim — "interactive tools have under 3% AIO
disruption" (E16) — rests on a single blocked page. The intent-split numbers (E9) come from vendor
stat-roundups I could not render either. The *direction* is corroborated four ways; the *magnitude* is not
verified. Treat "tool pages are AIO-resistant" as medium confidence and "under 3%" as unverified.

### 3. Being cited inside the AIO is the only way to win the informational surface, and it is not a product
Citation flips the sign: +35% organic CTR / ~120% more clicks per impression versus not being cited (E11).
But Google's own guidance (E6) says there is no lever for this beyond ordinary quality, crawlability and
structure — no file, no schema, no eligibility gate. So "AI-visibility optimisation" is a *practice* we
apply to our own pages, not an asset we can sell, and anything sold as a secret AI-ranking file is
snake oil.

### 4. `llms.txt` products are a deception trap
Google states flatly it does not use `llms.txt` for Search, AI Overviews or AI Mode (E6), and the file is
present on ~2.1% of the web with ~40% of those auto-generated by a WordPress plugin (E7). Selling
"llms.txt for Google AI visibility" would take money for a thing the vendor's own documentation says does
nothing. That is deceiving a buyer, which the constitution outranks the revenue target to forbid. **RED.**

### 5. Parasite SEO is closed
Google's 2024-11-19 clarification removed the escape hatch: hosting our content on someone else's
high-authority domain is site reputation abuse **regardless of editorial oversight or first-party
involvement** (E5). The "rent an aged domain's authority instead of waiting 4–8 months" shortcut (E21) is
not available. **RED.**

### 6. There is a real, tiny, non-Google channel: LLM citation without Google authority
25% of the top 1,000 ChatGPT-cited URLs had **zero** Google organic visibility (E13). That is the single
most interesting fact in this criterion for a new domain, because it says the 4–8 month Google authority
wait is not the only door. But E14 sizes the room honestly: AI referral volume is 47–190× smaller than
Google organic and AI platforms are ~0.15% of global web traffic. Better conversion on a rounding error is
still a rounding error. This is a **supplementary channel for an existing tool site**, not a line.

### 7. Getting paid by AI crawlers is the right shape and the wrong time
Pay-per-crawl / pay-per-inference is exactly the money model a software-only operation would want: no
buyer conversation, no human, machine-to-machine settlement. It fails on three separate gates today
(E17, E18): it is **closed beta with a human application step**, it **requires existing AI-crawler traffic**
that a new site does not have, and the payout rail is a dedicated Stripe account with minimum thresholds
that neither I nor two independent third-party research logs could tie to Israel. Cloudflare takes ~30%
(E19). **Re-check when it leaves beta** — the shape is right and the shape is rare.

## Dead ends and unclosed questions

- **Every Google primary source is behind the proxy.** `developers.google.com` is blocked in full. Every
  policy claim in this file is a third-party rendering of a Google page. The named primary URLs are in the
  ledger; a human or an unblocked agent should open E1, E5, E6 and E20's `developers.google.com` links.
- **Hebrew / Israel AI Overview coverage is UNKNOWN.** One search returned nothing on whether AIO or AI
  Mode runs on Hebrew-language queries in Israel, or at what rate. This is decisive for `il-biz-tools`
  and it is **not answerable by search** — it is answerable by running ten Hebrew queries on
  google.co.il and looking. That is a five-minute observation, and it is the highest-value single
  action anyone can take on this criterion.
- **No external buyer exists for anything in this criterion.** The criterion produces *constraints on how
  we build*, not a product. Every attempt to turn a policy insight into something someone pays for landed
  on either an agency service (needs a human) or snake oil (violates the constitution). That is a real,
  useful negative result: this criterion should not be re-swept looking for a line.
- **E16 ("under 3% AIO disruption for interactive tools") is unverified** and it is load-bearing for the
  group's whole thesis. `thedigitalbloom.com` and `serps.io` are both blocked.
- **Ad-supported content is dead for us and this criterion adds a second nail.** 68% zero-click (E12) sits
  on top of the sibling scout's finding that the ad-network ladder no longer has a bottom rung (E21).
