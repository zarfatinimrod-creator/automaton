### Paid GitHub App on GitHub Marketplace (global, businesses billed by GitHub)

- **Shape:** `B-compounding-history` · scout `b2b-invoice` · scout confidence 60/100 · **verified: no**
- **What it is:** Publish a free GitHub App under the brand org, accumulate the 100 installations GitHub's own docs name as the gate, then switch on a per-seat paid plan that GitHub bills to the customer's existing GitHub payment method and remits to us at 95%.
- **Who pays:** Businesses that already have a GitHub organization and an established GitHub billing relationship (card or invoiced enterprise account) — the buyer is an engineering org paying a software line item, not a consumer at a checkout.
- **Why they pay:** Because the app does a job inside repositories they already pay GitHub for, and buying it costs them one click on an account they already have — no procurement, no vendor onboarding, no new invoice relationship. The product this rail should carry is an obligation one: the EU Cyber Resilience Act's Article 14 duties went live on 11 September 2026 (24h early warning, 72h notification, 14 days after a fix) and machine-readable SBOMs become mandatory on 11 December 2027, so a per-release conformity-evidence archive that cannot be backfilled is both shape C and shape B. That product half is NOT yet measured and free CLI kits exist (see deadEnds) — the rail is the finding, the product needs its own occupancy wave.
- **How the first customer arrives:** Not through search ranking. GitHub's docs make the paid gate an explicitly countable number rather than an undisclosed ranking: 'GitHub Apps should have a minimum of 100 installations' before a paid plan may be published.
